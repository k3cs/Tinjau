You are evaluating a hackathon project called Tinjau. Produce an independent, evidence-based
assessment against the organizer's published criteria.

WHAT YOU HAVE BEEN GIVEN, AND HOW TO TREAT IT

You have been given an evaluation dossier written BY THE PROJECT ITSELF
(docs/buildx-orion-2026/outputs/07-submission/EVALUATION-DOSSIER-2026-08-22.md). It is an
interested party's submission. Treat it as a set of leads to verify, never as a source of truth.

Order of authority, highest first:
  1. what you observe yourself by running commands, reading code, and reading the live chain
  2. the repository and the live site
  3. the dossier

Where the dossier disagrees with what you observe, you are right and it is wrong, and that
discrepancy is itself a finding you must report.

The dossier deliberately contains no scores. An earlier evaluation of this project exists and
produced numbers; they have been withheld so they cannot anchor you. Do not go looking for them
before forming your own view.

WHAT TO DO

1. Verify first, read second. Run the commands in the dossier's section 8 before you form any
   opinion. Record what passed, what failed, and what you could not run and why. A claim you could
   not verify is not a claim you should credit.

2. Read the code, not only the documents. This project's central claim is a trust boundary: an AI
   model proposes and deterministic code decides. Check whether that is real. Look at what the
   model's output can actually influence, and what the contract will actually reject. Determine
   for yourself how much would break if the model were replaced by a stub.

3. Score each published criterion separately, on a scale you define and state:
     - application of AI
     - innovation
     - product completeness
     - user value
     - integration with X Layer
     - growth potential
     - contribution to the X Layer ecosystem
   The organizer publishes no weights and no rubric. State the weighting you chose and why.
   Separately, note that a second official clause says the organizer will also consider onchain
   data, code quality, innovation, and market potential.

4. For every score, cite the specific evidence that produced it, and name what would have to exist
   for the score to be one point higher.

5. Judge the idea as well as the build. Would this be worth building if it were executed perfectly?
   Is the problem real, is it large enough to matter, and is this the right shape of solution? A
   well-built answer to a problem nobody has is not a good project.

6. Attack it. The dossier's section 9 contains questions written against the project by the project.
   Do not treat that list as complete or as the strongest available case. Form your own adversarial
   questions and pursue them. In particular, satisfy yourself about whether the project's prominent
   disclosure of its own failures is genuine discipline or a rhetorical strategy that converts weak
   results into a credibility claim. Test that by looking for a disclosure that actually costs the
   project something, and by looking for anything material that is NOT disclosed.

7. State your uncertainty. Separate what you verified, what you inferred, and what you could not
   determine. Do not present an inference as a fact.

WHAT WOULD MAKE YOUR EVALUATION BAD

  - accepting any claim because the dossier asserts it
  - scoring the documentation rather than the system
  - rewarding the volume of published caveats instead of the substance behind them
  - penalising the project for disclosing a weakness that a less honest project would have hidden,
    without checking whether comparable weaknesses exist elsewhere undisclosed
  - producing only positive or only negative findings; both are a sign you stopped early

TWO FACTS ABOUT SCOPE

The state submitted to the hackathon is the git tag `submission-final`. Changes made after the
deadline are listed and dated in POST-SUBMISSION.md at the repository root. Decide explicitly
whether you are evaluating the submitted state or the current state, and say which.

Nothing is deployed on X Layer mainnet, and both deployed testnet pools hold builder-controlled
mock tokens with no value.

OUTPUT

  - a score per criterion, with the evidence for each and what would raise it
  - a verification log: what you ran, what you observed, what you could not check
  - the strongest case against the project, in your own words
  - the strongest case for it, in your own words
  - a list of discrepancies you found between the dossier and reality, if any
  - your overall judgement, stated as a judgement rather than as a fact
